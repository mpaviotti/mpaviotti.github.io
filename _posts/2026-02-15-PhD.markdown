# 🎓 What a PhD Really Is

A PhD (Doctor of Philosophy) is a research degree focused on creating **new
knowledge**, not just learning existing knowledge.

It is the **highest academic degree** awarded by universities — held by roughly **2% of the world’s population**.  

At the same time, **failure rates for PhD students can be as high as 40%** (based on my calculations at a university I worked for).  

It typically consists of:

- **Deep specialization** in a narrow topic  
- **Original research** that makes a significant contribution  
- **Independent work** under supervision  
- **Publishing and presenting** your findings  
- Writing and defending a **doctoral thesis**

Earning a PhD means demonstrating that you:

- Possess **deep knowledge** of your field  
- Can engage with **international experts** as a peer  
- Have made a **significant and original contribution** to knowledge

**In short:** PhD is training to become an independent researcher capable of
producing and defending original knowledge.

## 🌟 It can be the best moment of your life,... :pirate_flag: or the worst!
I'll put it in a sentence: 

`Doing a PhD, is not for the faint-hearted.`  

A PhD can become, one of your **biggest life achievements** and the most **intellectually rewarding periods** of your life.
This provided that you can make **consistent progress**, tolerate uncertainty, reach the finish line. 

Otherwise, it can become **a long tunnel with no visible exit**, a slow erosion of confidence, and a prolonged period of stress and doubt. 

You might think I’m trying to scare you --- partly true --- but, better to be
honest than lure you into a long tunnel that’s hard to exit.

## 🔥 If You're Not Failing, You're Not Doing a PhD

A PhD is not about constant success, it is more about failure than success. 

I personally think I failed in my life more than I succeded, but I learned my lessons.  

Failure is not a side effect, it is **the mechanism.** If you cannot tolerate being wrong, uncertain, or behind for long periods, a PhD will feel unbearable.
  
In doing a PhD you will:

- Submit papers that get rejected 📄❌  
- Run experiments that fail 🧪💥  
- Write code that doesn’t work 💻🐛  
- Read things you don’t understand (yet) 🤯  
- Question whether you belong 😶

But, 💪 if you can treat failure as data, confusion as progress, and rejection as part of life, then a PhD is for you. In other words, if failure challenges you and drives you forward, and if not knowing things excites you rather than frustrates you, then this path is for you.

## 🧠 AI won't help you get it 
The harsh truth is that undergraduate degrees are getting easier and easier to attain and PhD are **as demanding as ever**. 

In the modern age of AI-generated degrees, undergraduates are often **less
trained to work independently** and to push themselves rigorously. On the other
hand, PhD requirements haven’t changed over time. 

Moreover, while it is possible to pass an undergrad exam using AI, the same is not true for a PhD.  **ChatGPT or any AI won’t earn you a PhD**. AI can regurgitate existing knowledge, but a PhD thesis requires **original work that has never been done before**. No shortcut exists here — you have to create, not just repeat.  

Also, during an undergraduate degree, you are **safeguarded**; Material is prepared for you, grades follow standardized metrics, and admission almost guarantees eventual graduation.  

A PhD, on the other hand, **does not follow these rules**; There is **no guarantee of success**.

# 🔱 Let's be real: The most important thing of a PhD

Let's be real: 

`life is a balance of giving and taking`. ⚖️

Relationships are not built on magic or illusions — they are built on mutual benefit.🤝  

Two people enter a relationship because **both gain something meaningful** from it.  
Any connection that ignores this fundamental balance risks becoming **toxic**. ☠️🧪⚠️

This basic life lesson is the core in the PhD program and therefore I'd say that the most important in your PhD is  

`The relationship with your supervisor`. 

So it is crucial  that you find a supervisor whose  **research interests align** with yours, someone you feel **comfortable working with** and someone whose mentoring style matches your needs. 

## 🤝 The 2-Player Game

A PhD is essentially a **two-player game** between the student and the supervisor.   

Crucially though, if this relationship break, the one who suffers the most is the student.🛋️💡 
While the supervisor is responsible for you, if you do not carry on with your own work, they will not lose their job if you don't get your PhD. 

In many European systems in particular, the **supervisor may be the single most important factor** in your PhD experience.

A bad or toxic supervisor can derail even a strong student, while a good supervisor can elevate an average project into an excellent one. Still a good supervisor can do little if the students is not willing to contribute.
